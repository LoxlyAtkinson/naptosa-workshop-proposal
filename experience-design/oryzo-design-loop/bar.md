# Design Loop bar — Oryzo interaction principles adapted for NAPTOSA

Reference: [ORYZO AI](https://oryzo.ai/). The audit captured 42 wheel/key scroll states and the 12 section anchors at 1440×900 (see `oryzo-review/`). Use its storytelling and interaction mechanics as the craft bar; do not reuse its brand identity, palette, typography, wording, product fiction, imagery, or exact layouts.

1. **Build a complete opening frame before the scroll begins.** Oryzo opens on a composed tabletop/product scene with a large product wordmark, two short lines of copy, a persistent header, and a visible prompt to continue. NAPTOSA must open with its own human, national-to-provincial proposal image and a concise claim; headline, PDF action, and chapter navigation must remain legible in the static opening state.

2. **Give the reader a real reveal, then carry the story through distinct acts.** Oryzo moves from a tabletop still life to a floating product, then AI joke, wearable editorial, physical feature scenes, technical demo, social proof, options, and a final return to its opening hook. NAPTOSA's corresponding arc is shared purpose → connected provinces → what is being built → workshop capability → safe, separate data → commercial value → delivery → approval.

3. **Change the visual device when the idea changes.** The reference alternates an immersive 3D object scene, editorial portrait gallery, full-scene workbench, technical animation, testimonial scene, and product-choice surface. NAPTOSA must use at least five distinct devices across its ten chapters (layered hero, geographic network, editorial people imagery, process/flow diagram, comparison, price composition, delivery path); the same device cannot lead consecutive chapters.

4. **Hold a motif across scene changes.** Oryzo carries the same physical coaster through the tabletop, floating reveal, wearable gallery, feature demonstrations, and close. NAPTOSA should carry a distinctive, brand-correct network motif (provincial nodes/connecting line, or educator portrait framing) between chapters so the visual cuts still feel like one proposal. Do not imply provinces share records when the approved option separates databases.

5. **Make demonstrations respond to the reader.** The reference presents visible temperature-slider, flip-encryption, product-choice, and play controls. NAPTOSA needs at least one meaningful, reversible interaction in each core explanatory act (network/workshop/data/pricing) that changes a diagram or reveals a comparison; each control must have a clear label, keyboard operation, and visible selected state. Decorative animation alone does not count.

6. **Make scroll animation tell the chapter's point.** Across 42 captured scroll states, Oryzo moves and transforms its subject and cuts into distinct scenes, rather than only fading headings. NAPTOSA's progress must reverse cleanly when scrolling upward and settle at stable states; movement must demonstrate the current idea (provinces connect, workshop steps progress, data separates, totals resolve) and never cover copy or prices.

7. **Make the ending resolve the opening.** Oryzo re-states its opening proposition at the end, then leaves a clear route into its product detail. NAPTOSA's closing act should echo the opening network, then give one direct acceptance/download action. Preserve the user's price rules in every scene: changed offers show a legible original rate and spotlight the yellow offered rate; unchanged rates stay unchanged.

8. **Keep all content available without motion.** At desktop and narrow mobile widths, and with reduced motion enabled, every chapter, price, label, interaction destination and next step remains readable and usable. Never rely on color or animation alone to communicate a distinction. Diagrams require text equivalents and accessible contrast.
