## Verdicts

### Mobile copy contrast and fit — PASS
**Evidence:** In `network-film-mobile.png`, the yellow kicker and white heading and paragraph sit on a solid navy panel with a yellow edge. The full copy remains inside the panel, is legible at the shown 390 px width, and does not collide with the persistent navigation button. The heading wraps cleanly to three short lines.

### Reduced-motion fallback readability — PASS (visual only)
**Evidence:** `network-film-reduced.png` shows the same complete message on a static image, with no text clipped and the same strong local panel contrast. The still image supports a usable static presentation. A screenshot alone cannot prove that the video was not fetched or that motion is actually disabled.

### Desktop scene composition — PASS with a notable story gap
**Evidence:** In `network-film-late.png`, the copy panel is prominent against the full-width scene and all visible supporting text is readable. The chapter rail stays outside the copy panel. The lower edge of the kicker is cropped by the screenshot boundary, so this particular captured position does not show the full label.

### Human-centered visual storytelling — FAIL (moderate)
**Evidence:** Both `network-film-mobile.png` and `network-film-reduced.png` are dominated by a laptop, documents, and a tabletop; no educators are visible. The visible “One NAPTOSA community” copy therefore promises a human community that the shown mobile scene does not deliver. `network-film-late.png` likewise shows mostly the table and documents in the visible portion. This weakens the intended feel-good NAPTOSA moment and leaves the new scene reading as generic office footage.

**Achievable improvement:** Reframe the video for the mobile viewport so educators remain visible, or choose a source whose main subject is the educators and collaboration throughout the scrub range. Keep the static poster on that same people-forward frame.

### Scroll progression — UNVERIFIED from this image-only packet
The two desktop samples are not enough to establish continuous progression or clean reversal. They show distinct positions, but do not establish the intermediate motion. Preserve browser time/scroll measurements as functional evidence.
